About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Software Engineering Laboratory at the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Osaka. My research spans two connected fields: software engineering and AI for software engineering (AI4SE). My goal is to make software more reliable and secure by understanding how developers build, test and maintain it, and by creating tools that help them do it better.

In software engineering, I study software testing and fuzzing, software security and reliability, and open-source ecosystems through large-scale empirical studies: why fuzzers fail to build, how open-source communities adopt fuzz testing, what a million continuous-fuzzing sessions reveal about its effectiveness, and how open-source projects cope when core developers leave.

In AI4SE, I explore how large language models and AI agents can test, debug and repair software, from repairing quantum programs with the help of mutation analysis to understanding how generative AI will reshape developer environments. Each field informs the other: empirical insight into real-world development grounds new AI tools, and AI opens new ways to tackle long-standing software engineering problems.

I received my Ph.D. from Kyushu University in 2024 (early completion, advised by Prof. Yasutaka Kamei), including a research visit to USI Lugano, and my bachelor's degree in Software Engineering from Concordia University (Montréal). Before academia, I worked as a software quality engineer at Apple Japan, building machine-learning pipelines to triage bug reports and test failures.
