About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Software Engineering Laboratory at the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Osaka. I work in software engineering, with a focus on AI for software engineering (AI4SE): how large language models and AI agents can help developers test, debug and repair software, and where they still fall short.

My current work explores LLM- and agent-based software testing and automated program repair, from repairing quantum programs with the help of mutation analysis to understanding how generative AI will reshape developer environments. I combine building such techniques with large-scale empirical studies, so that new AI tools are grounded in how software is actually developed and maintained.

Much of my background is in software testing and fuzzing and empirical software engineering: why fuzzers fail to build, how open-source communities adopt fuzz testing, what a million continuous-fuzzing sessions reveal about its effectiveness, and how open-source ecosystems evolve and stay healthy. These studies inform my broader goal of making software more reliable and secure.

I received my Ph.D. from Kyushu University in 2024 (early completion, advised by Prof. Yasutaka Kamei), including a research visit to USI Lugano, and my bachelor's degree in Software Engineering from Concordia University (Montréal). Before academia, I worked as a software quality engineer at Apple Japan, building machine-learning pipelines to triage bug reports and test failures.
